# Zapier vs Make: which automation tool to start with

Zapier and Make both move data between the apps you already pay for. They are not two versions of the same product, and the choice between them is not about which one is better. It is about the shape of the work you are automating.

## The difference that decides it

Zapier is built around the connection. It has the widest catalogue of app integrations, so the awkward tool nobody else supports usually has a connector here. A Zap is a straight line: this happens, then that happens.

Make is built around the scenario. It puts the whole flow on one visual canvas, so branching, filters, and data shaping stay visible while you build them. The first hour is slower than dragging out a simple two-step automation. What you get back is a flow that can grow without being rebuilt from scratch.

The practical split: Zapier wins when you need an app connected at all. Make wins when the logic between the apps is the hard part.

## Pick Zapier when

- The apps are the problem, not the logic. You need your form, your client notes, and your scheduling tool to agree with each other, and none of them talk to each other today.
- The automations are short. Two to five steps, triggered by one event, occasionally with a single condition attached.
- Someone who is not technical has to keep it running. A linear Zap is readable to anyone on the team, and that matters more than it sounds.

## Pick Make when

- Branching is the point. Different customers, different paths, different follow-up, all inside one flow you can look at.
- You are already chaining automations to work around a limit. That is the clearest signal there is. When you build a second automation to compensate for the first, the work has outgrown the tool.
- You want to see the whole flow at once. Debugging a long automation is easier when the routing is drawn rather than described.

## The cost nobody puts on the pricing page

Both tools charge you in maintenance, not only in money. Every automation you build is something that can break when a vendor changes an API or renames a field. The teams that get real value from either tool keep a small number of flows that matter and delete the rest.

Whichever one you choose, name each flow after the job it does, not the apps it touches. "New client intake" survives an app change. "Form to Sheets" does not.

## When to skip both

If one person runs the business and the manual step takes four minutes a week, automating it is a hobby rather than an efficiency gain. Automate the step you do every day and get wrong.

There is also a lower-tech option worth one honest minute of your time: most tools now ship a native integration with the two or three apps people actually pair them with. If your need is covered there, you do not need either product yet.

## Before you commit

Check the vendor's own integration list for the specific apps you depend on, and read the current pricing page. Both companies change their catalogues and their plan limits regularly, and any comparison written a year ago may not describe the plan you are buying today.

## Bottom line

Start with Zapier if the job is connecting apps. Move to Make when the logic between those apps becomes the work. Do not run both unless you can state the reason in one sentence.
